Liver-Friendly Diet

Food is one of the most powerful levers for liver health, especially for MASLD (fatty liver).

Stress, Cortisol & Carbohydrate Metabolism

Diet isn't only about what's on your plate — how your body handles stress changes how it processes the carbohydrates you eat, too. Here's the chain of events, in plain terms.

1

Stress triggers cortisol release. Physical or psychological stress prompts your adrenal glands to release cortisol, the body's main stress hormone.

↓
2

Cortisol tells the liver to make more glucose. It activates liver enzymes that break down stored glycogen (glycogenolysis) and build new glucose from other sources (gluconeogenesis) — a short-term "fight or flight" response that raises blood sugar so you have fuel on hand.

↓
3

Occasional stress is fine — chronic stress isn't. When cortisol stays elevated day after day rather than settling back down, blood sugar runs higher than it needs to, and the pancreas keeps releasing extra insulin to bring it back into range.

↓
4

Cells stop responding well to insulin. Persistently high cortisol and insulin can drive insulin resistance, meaning more sugar and insulin circulate in the blood without being used efficiently by muscle and fat tissue.

↓
5

The liver converts the excess into fat. Facing more sugar and insulin than it can use, the liver ramps up de novo lipogenesis (turning excess carbohydrate into fat), takes in more fat released from other tissues, and burns less fat for fuel — a combination that drives fat build-up inside the liver itself.

This is one reason researchers now study chronic, unmanaged stress as a contributing factor in MASLD (fatty liver), alongside diet, alcohol, and activity level. Managing stress — sleep, movement, and relaxation practices — is a liver-health lever too, not just what's on your plate.

Leafy Greens

Spinach, kale, and arugula are rich in glutathione — an antioxidant that supports the liver's detox pathways.

Cruciferous Vegetables

Broccoli, cauliflower, and Brussels sprouts support the liver's natural detox enzyme activity.

Fatty Fish

Salmon, mackerel, and sardines provide omega-3s that can help reduce liver fat and inflammation.

Nuts & Seeds

Walnuts and flaxseeds bring antioxidants and healthy fats that support liver enzyme activity.

Limit

Added Sugar

Fructose is metabolized almost exclusively by the liver — excess sugar is a key driver of fatty liver.

Limit

Processed Foods

Trans fats, preservatives, and high sodium make the liver work harder. Cook fresh meals where you can.
