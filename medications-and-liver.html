Medications & Supplements: What Can Strain Your Liver

This is different from the Supplements page — this is general awareness of substances linked to liver strain, not a shopping guide. Never stop or change a prescribed medication based on this page; talk to your prescribing doctor first.

High Risk (Overdose)

Acetaminophen / Paracetamol

Safe at recommended doses, but the single most common cause of acute liver failure when taken in excess — risk rises further when combined with alcohol.

Use With Care

NSAIDs

Ibuprofen, naproxen, and diclofenac at high doses or with long-term use — especially combined with alcohol — carry added liver risk.

Monitored

Certain Antibiotics

Amoxicillin-clavulanate (Augmentin) is a leading antibiotic cause of drug-induced liver injury, usually from an immune-type reaction.

Monitored

Antiseizure Medications

Phenytoin and carbamazepine can occasionally trigger immune-related liver inflammation, more often early in treatment.

Monitored

Methotrexate

Used for autoimmune conditions and some cancers — long-term use, especially with alcohol, is linked to liver inflammation and scarring, so routine monitoring is standard practice.

Monitored

Antifungal Medications

Fluconazole, itraconazole, and ketoconazole can affect the liver with extended use, ranging from mild enzyme changes to rarer, more serious injury.

Higher Risk

Herbal & "Natural" Supplements

Kava, black cohosh, comfrey, and high-dose green tea extract are among the herbal products most consistently linked to liver injury — "natural" doesn't mean the risk is zero.

Usually Safe

Statins

Widely and safely used long-term. They can cause a temporary rise in liver enzymes, which typically normalizes — routine bloodwork is precautionary, not usually a cause for alarm.

What to actually do with this: if you take any regular medication, ask your doctor or pharmacist whether it's one that benefits from periodic liver-function bloodwork (ALT, AST, GGT) — most drug-related liver injury is caught early exactly that way, long before symptoms appear. Never stop a prescribed medication on your own based on this page.
