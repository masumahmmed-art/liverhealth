Risk Factors

Some risks are lifestyle-driven and modifiable; others are genetic or viral. Knowing yours helps you decide when to get tested.

High Risk

Excess Alcohol

Regular heavy drinking is a leading cause of liver disease. Even moderate drinking adds strain over time.

High Risk

Obesity & Diabetes

Metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD) affects about 3 in 10 adults worldwide and is often silent until advanced. Source

Moderate

Viral Hepatitis

Hepatitis B and C are major causes of cirrhosis and liver cancer. Vaccines exist for Hepatitis A and B.

Moderate

Family History

Genetic conditions like hemochromatosis or Wilson's disease raise risk — know your family health history.

Moderate

Certain Medications

Long-term use of some painkillers, antibiotics, and herbal supplements can affect the liver.

Protective

Regular Exercise

150 minutes of moderate exercise weekly is linked to notably lower fatty-liver risk. Movement is medicine.

About This Site
LiverHealthHub is an independent educational project — it isn't a medical practice, clinic, or affiliated with any hospital or health system. It's maintained by one person, motivated by how common and how preventable most liver disease actually is, with the goal of turning scattered medical research into plain-language guidance people can actually use.

The content is researched and written by summarizing peer-reviewed studies and guidance from major health authorities (see the Research page for dated, sourced summaries and the citation behind the Liver Fat Estimate calculator) — it is not written or reviewed by a licensed medical professional, and nothing here should be treated as personalized medical advice.

Built with reference to how major health publishers (Mayo Clinic, Healthline, NHS, Liver UK) structure liver-health content: symptom guides, risk breakdowns, and dated, sourced research summaries.
